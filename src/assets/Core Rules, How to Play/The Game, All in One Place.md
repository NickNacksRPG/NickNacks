This page is my attempt to condense most of the major rules in the game. Many sections will not include all of the information on their respective topics, but will instead give you an idea on how each one functions, with a link to the page for further reading.

Nick Nacks uses a d20 as the dice used for most action resolution.
## <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 80)">SECTION 1: STATS</span> 

## <span style="color:rgb(103, 254, 120)">Ability Scores</span>
The building blocks of all other stats, ability scores are foundational to nearly every other aspect of the game. There are 6 total, each with a general purpose.

There are 3 stats that I would consider "primary stats" that most characters will invest in at least 1
[[STR]] - Brute strength. Affects attacking, breaking things, carrying capacity, armor
[[DEX]] - Agility. Affects stealth, thievery, attacking with an emphasis on ranged attacking
[[INT]] - Smarts. Affects knowledge, number of skills, techniques and how powerful your spells are.

There are 3 support stats that augment other aspects of the game or give new abilities
[[LUK]] - Lucky! A generalist stat that supports other stats, particularly good for skills.
[[WIS]] - Faith. Wis gives access to powers depending on the god you worship
[[CHA]] - Teamwork. Buffs companion creatures and makes you better at team play

<span style="color:rgb(192, 0, 0)">Read More: </span>[[Ability Scores|Ability Score]]
## <span style="color:rgb(103, 254, 120)">HP</span>
Stands for hit points. HP represents how much punishment your character can take before you are [[defeated]]. All creatures start off with a base HP value of 20, then receive more each level depending on [[+Class]] (or equivalent of class for bestiary creatures)

<span style="color:rgb(192, 0, 0)">Read More:</span> [[+HP]]
## <span style="color:rgb(103, 254, 120)">AC</span>
AC stands for Armor Class and its the stat that determines how hard you are to hit with attack rolls. It is a target number enemies are shooting for in order to hit you with swords. When an attack matches or exceeds this number, it is considered a hit. When an attack exceeds this number by 10 or more, it is a [[crit]]. When an attack is lower than this number, but not by more than 5, it is considered a [[graze]]: Technically a miss, but still deals half damage, and doesn't apply bonus [[On-hit]] effects.

Most [[+Status|status effects]] in Nick Nacks do not adjust your AC, and instead give the attacker a bonus to their attack roll. This is mostly to keep the target number consistent.

<span style="color:rgb(192, 0, 0)">Read More: </span>[[AC|AC]]
## <span style="color:rgb(103, 254, 120)">Poise</span> 
Poise represents how hard you are to push around. Poise functions less like AC and more like HP in that it is a pool that is depleted. When a creature attempts a [[+Combat Maneuver|combat maneuver]] against you, they deal "Poise Damage" equal to their attack roll. If this reduces your poise to 0 or lower, then their maneuver is successful and your poise resets back to full. 

<span style="color:rgb(192, 0, 0)">Read More:</span> [[Poise]]
## <span style="color:rgb(103, 254, 120)">Saving Throws</span>
Saving throws, often called saves, are a type of dice roll you make in response to an opponents action that is not an attack roll. Many spells involve saving throws. The target number is typically (12+[[LvL]] of creature inflicting the effect) They come in three forms.
[[Fortitude]] (fort) - Attacks on the body that require sturdiness or a healthy body
[[Reflex]] (refl) - Attacks on the body that require quick reactions and dodging
[[Will]] - Attacks on the mind, such as to control it or strike fear

<span style="color:rgb(192, 0, 0)">Read More:</span> [[+Saving Throw|Saves]]
## <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 80)">SECTION 2: ATTACKING</span>

## <span style="color:rgb(103, 254, 120)">Attack Rolls</span>
Attack is the general term used to describe the bonus you apply to your attack rolls. Anything that involves you rolling a dice is an [[Attack Roll]]. There are numerous types of attack rolls, each with their own unique interactions...
[[Melee Attack]] - An attack made against a creature within your reach that does not involve some kind of projectile.
[[Ranged Attack]] - An attack against a creature with some kind of projectile. Comes in a few unique forms.
[[Spell Attack]] - An attack roll that is made as part of casting a spell. Can be melee or ranged.
[[Natural Attack]] - An attack made with a creatures body that is designed to be a weapon, like a claw, bite or club-like tail.
[[Unarmed Attack]] - An attack made with a creatures body that isn't necessarily a weapon, but thanks to special training, is one. Includes punches, kicks, headbutts and other parts of the body.
[[Weapon Attack]] - An attack made with a manufactured weapon, like a sword, shield or crossbow.
[[+Siege Weapon]] - A special type of attack originating from a weapon of war or an abnormally large weapon, such as a [[Ballista]] or an enormous rock.

<span style="color:rgb(192, 0, 0)">Read More:</span> [[+Types of Attack Rolls]]
## <span style="color:rgb(103, 254, 120)">Combat Maneuvers</span>
Combat maneuvers are [[Attack Roll]]s that are focused more on pushing your enemy around than dealing direct HP damage. When you make a combat maneuver you are attacking, and this deals [[Poise]] damage, and is considered damage for effects that mention it. Combat maneuvers have special interactions with [[Size]] than can potentially prevent lesser creatures from tossing larger ones around. There are some oddball maneuvers, but here are the standard options available to all creatures.
[[Bully]] - Deal poise damage with the hopes of setting up an ally
[[Disarm]] - Remove an opponent's weapon or item from their hand
[[Drag]] - Force a creature to follow you by dragging them
[[Grapple]] - Hold a creature still
[[Dirty Trick]] - Do something unorthodox to impede your foes movement, vision, attacks etc
[[Shove]] - Push a creature directly away from you
[[Topple]] - Knock a creature down
<span style="color:rgb(192, 0, 0)">Read More:</span> [[+Combat Maneuver]]
## <span style="color:rgb(103, 254, 120)">Special vs Simple Attacks</span> 
Nick Nacks makes purposeful distinctions between certain forms of attack for balance reasons. One such important distinction is special attack and simple attack. Special attacks are the types of attacks that let you do all kinds of awesome things, and can even include special variants of [[+Combat Maneuver]]. Simple attacks are the basic attack and damage type attacks. <span style="color:rgb(192, 0, 0)">You can only perform special attacks during your turn, and only once per turn, even if you have multiple attacks</span>. This is to prevent scenarios where someone can [[Base Special Attacks|cleave]] on an [[AoO]] and attack multiple creatures nearby multiple times per turn and off-turn.

<span style="color:rgb(192, 0, 0)">Read More:</span> [[Simple Attack]] and [[Special Attack]]
## <span style="color:rgb(103, 254, 120)">Attacks of Opportunity</span>
The most common form of [[simple attack]]. An attack of opportunity is an attack typically made against a creature for performing an "unsafe" action. The most common action is moving through spaces you [[threaten]]. Special features can allow you to use your AoOs for additional things, or to force a creature to provoke for you and your allies. You can only make one AoO each round.

<span style="color:rgb(192, 0, 0)">Read More:</span> [[AoO|Attack of Opportunity]], [[Threaten]], [[Reach]]
## <span style="color:rgb(103, 254, 120)">On-Hit Effects</span>
Many features surrounding attacking mention the "oh-hit" terminology. On-hit effects are bonus effects tied to the attack and are contingent on landing the attacl. A hit requires that you successfully meet or exceed a creatures AC and deal damage. As such, a [[graze]] does not apply.

## <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 80)">SECTION 3: ACTIONS</span>

## <span style="color:rgb(103, 254, 120)">Action Types</span>
Nick Nacks has a hard action system that assigns all abilities to specific types of actions. All actions can be used in between each other (ie move, attack, move), allowing for fluid turns that can update a plan of attack as things happen.

The main actions are as follows...
[[Standard Action]] - You get 1 standard action each turn. Most actions in the game require a standard action, such as a [[Multiattack]] or casting spells.
[[Swift Action]] - An additional action you gain each round to perform some kind of lesser effect during your turn, like activating a [[Buff]] or [[Jump|jumping]].
[[Movement]] - Movement is not necessarily an action, but each character can move their speed each turn. Some abilities allow you to "spend" movement 
[[Free Action]] - Simple actions that should only be limited by physics (ie opening a door is easy once, but not 1,000 times).

Secondary actions are variants on these actions.
[[Full-Round Action]] - A FRA requires your standard action, and it consumes all of your movement, making the action much more restrictive. The most common action is [[charge]].
[[Immediate Action]] - Immediate actions are swift actions, but they can be triggered when it isn't your turn. Many counter-style features are immediate instead of swift. You still can only use one swift or immediate action each turn.
[[Instant Action]] - A free action that can be taken off-turn. The most common form is the [[AoO]].

<span style="color:rgb(192, 0, 0)">Read More:</span> [[Action System]]
## <span style="color:rgb(103, 254, 120)">Standard Actions</span>
Here is a list of common standard actions anyone can do and a simple description of what they do.
[[Administer Potion]] - Feed another creature a [[+Potions|potion]]
[[Aid]] - Give an ally bonuses, or help them stave off minor [[+Status]]
[[Carry]] - Pick up an ally and move them around
[[Dash]] - Double your movement this turn
[[Disengage]] - Make your movement [[Elusive]] this turn, protecting you from [[AoO]]s
[[Focus]] - Pick one enemy to get bonuses against, while getting debuffed against all other enemies.
[[Prepare]] - Ready a specific action in response to a trigger that happens off-turn
[[Total Defense]] - Reset your [[Poise]] and gain defensive stats
## <span style="color:rgb(103, 254, 120)">Swift/Immediate Actions</span>
Here is a list of common swift actions anyone can do and a simple description of what they do.
[[Use Potion]] (swift) - Drink a helpful concoction
[[Apply Toxin]] (swift) - Apply a dangerous concoction to a weapon
[[Get Down!]] (immediate) - Automatically take the hit for a defeated creature, suffering all the negative effects.
[[Jump]] (swift) - Leap across gaps and over hazards. Does not grant additional movement.
[[Mount]] (swift) - Hop on another creature to ride them. You can dismount as an immediate action.
## <span style="color:rgb(103, 254, 120)">Free Actions</span>
[[Swap Item]] - At the start of your turn, decide what equipment is in your hands
[[Delay]] - Wait to act in initiative. You can take your turn at the end of any other turn.
## <span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 80)">SECTION 4: TECHNIQUES, MANA AND MORE</span>

## <span style="color:rgb(103, 254, 120)">Techniques</span>
Techniques are one of the main forms of progression in Nick Nacks. They are attacks, spells buffs and such that augment your character and let them do special things. All characters can technically access all techniques, but they require investment of [[Technique Point|technique points]] to gain access to higher level tech. You get access to techniques whose level is equal to the number of points you have invested in a particular tech tree. Techniques are separated into 2 categories, Martial and Magical. Martial tech tends to be much more focused on attacks, while magical tech has much more variety.

Techniques come in all types of actions, so to stay balanced, a hard cap is placed on how much tech you can use per turn. That cap is 2 "levels" of tech per turn, with the cap increasing by 1 each odd level after 1. For the purposes of this cap, [[upcast]] martial tech is considered a higher level. Note that this cap is not tied to mana, since that can be increased with [[+Status]] and decreased with [[Buff|buffs]], or outright ignored with [[Innate Technique|innate techniques]].

Speaking of, innate techniques are tech that ignore some of the rules surrounding techniques. Innate spells do not require a [[Staff Component|spell focus]]. Innate tech in general does not cost mana. Innate tech ignores the technique point system. For the most part, innate tech is almost exclusive given to bestiary creatures to make them more simple for the [[GM]] to run, but they can sometimes be granted by items or [[+Class]].

All martial techniques with [[Special Attack|special attacks]] have the [[Martial]] tag, and this tag is not listed in the techniques. The martial tag basically just means that no matter the attack form mentioned within the ability: a bite, a throw, a headbutt, a kick, the attack still does the damage of whatever weapon is being held in the hand, as though you were using your sword (or what have you) to initiate the attack.

Magical techniques, or [[Spell|spells]], have slightly more complicated rules. Spells require a [[Staff Component|spell focus]], an item held in the hand, typically a staff, in order to cast and concentrate on spells. Spells also have a verbal component, requiring the caster to be able to speak.

## <span style="color:rgb(103, 254, 120)">Mana</span>
Mana, lore-wise, is an animating force in all things. Mechanically, its a resource your characters uses in order to activate [[Technique Rules|techniques]], items and class abilities. All creatures posses a mana pool of (2+2*[[LvL]]+[[INT]]) at a baseline. Techniques cost mana equal to their level. You restore 1 mana per turn while not in combat (or something similar like a [[+Chase|chases]]), or 10 per minute.

Some features allow you to optionally use mana in more powerful ways that decrease your pool called mana burn. Mana burn is healed with rest, but otherwise is hard to remove.

<span style="color:rgb(192, 0, 0)">Read More: </span>[[Mana]], [[Mana Burn]]
## <span style="color:rgb(103, 254, 120)">Memory</span>
Memory is the stat that determines how many [[Technique Rules|techniques]] you can have prepared at once. At the start of the day, you decide to prepare a number of techniques equal to your memory. These techniques can be drawn from any tree you have [[Technique Point|technique points]] invested in. 

How much memory you have is determined by you class, and also your [[INT]].
